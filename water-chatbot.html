UXR Case Study · Academic + Live Project

Water Chatbot

An AI assistant answering Arizona residents' water questions — built on real user research, not guesswork.

Duration
4 months
Focus
UXR, Conversational UI, Research Documentation

Introduction

The Water Chatbot addresses water-related questions for Arizona residents. It was developed by a research team at Arizona State University, led by Dr. Claire Lauer, to help people find reliable water information quickly.

Why this project

I worked with the research team to define what the chatbot needed to know to genuinely help people — understanding what information to feed it based on real user needs.

Approach

We combined surveys, interviews, competitive analysis, and usability testing to shape both the chatbot's content and its tone.

Research methods

Survey forms

Understanding curiosity

Surveyed long-term Arizona residents to learn what they'd actually ask a water chatbot.

Heuristic evaluation

Benchmarking existing chatbots

Evaluated ChatGPT and Google Bard against Nielsen's heuristics to learn what tone and structure worked.

Competitive analysis

Mapping existing information

Reviewed existing water-info sources to judge credibility and structure content hierarchy.

Card sorting

Prioritizing survey data

Grouped survey questions by hand to surface the most-asked topics by category.

User interviews

Understanding pain points

Direct interviews on water concerns — notes and keywords shaped the eventual user persona.

User persona

A focal point for design

A realistic composite of key users, giving the team one focal point for every decision.

Usability testing materials

Preparing an unbiased test

Scripted the test in advance to catch biased questions before testing real users.

User testing

Observing real interaction

Think-aloud sessions with real users surfaced pain points directly.

Data breakdown

Finding patterns

Grouped findings into categories to guide the beta version of the chatbot.

Key insights

Turning findings into fixes

Translated research into clear, actionable recommendations for the chatbot's next version.

Conclusion

All research was documented and organized in Airtable — an open resource for anyone building the next version of the chatbot, or a similar conversational-AI project.
